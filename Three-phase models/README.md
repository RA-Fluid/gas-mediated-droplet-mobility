# Three-Phase Continuum and L-BGK Models

This folder contains MATLAB implementations of two three-phase models
for momentum transport through a nanometric gas layer surrounding a
rising oil droplet:

1. **Three-phase continuum model**
2. **Three-phase linearised Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook model**

Both models represent the gas layer as confined between two mobile
liquid interfaces:

- the inner oil-air interface; and
- the outer air-water interface.

The gas response is coupled separately to the internal oil circulation
and external water flow. The physical gas-layer thickness is inferred
by matching the modelled droplet drag to the compound-droplet buoyancy
at the measured terminal rise velocity.

## Model outputs

For each experimental observation, the models can calculate:

- equivalent gas-layer thickness, `delta_a` [m];
- gas-film Knudsen number, `Kn` [-];
- equivalent hydrodynamic slip length, `b_eq` [m];
- oil-air interfacial velocity amplitude, `A_o` [m/s];
- air-water interfacial velocity amplitude, `A_w` [m/s];
- tangential gas-pressure-gradient amplitude, `G` [Pa/m];
- relative tangential gas flux, `q_relative` [m^2/s];
- gas traction at the oil-air interface [Pa];
- gas traction at the air-water interface [Pa];
- predicted drag and compound-droplet buoyancy [N];
- force-balance residuals and numerical validity checks.

The reported `delta_a` should be interpreted as a model-inferred
equivalent gas-layer thickness. The models do not resolve the local
angular thickness distribution around the droplet.

The reported `b_eq` is the Feng-model slip length that reproduces the
total drag predicted by the coupled three-phase model. It is a global
drag-equivalent quantity, not a molecular velocity-jump length at
either gas-liquid interface.

## Gas-transport closures

Both the continuum and L-BGK models support the same two limiting
gas-transport closures.

### 1. Pressure-gradient-free limit

```matlab
options.gasClosure = 'zero-pressure-gradient';
```

This condition imposes:

```text
G = 0
```

The relative tangential gas flux is calculated as a model output and is
generally nonzero.

This closure represents a locally pressure-gradient-free gas film in
which tangential gas transport is permitted. Redistribution of that gas
elsewhere around the droplet is not resolved by the local model.

### 2. Zero-relative-flux limit

```matlab
options.gasClosure = 'closed-shell';
```

This condition imposes:

```text
q_relative = 0
```

The pressure-gradient amplitude `G` is calculated so that the
pressure-driven gas response offsets the transport generated by the
relative motion of the two interfaces.

This closure represents a steady, uniform attached-shell limit.

The experiments do not directly resolve global gas redistribution.
The two closures should therefore be interpreted as limiting cases.

## Continuum model

The three-phase continuum formulation describes transport within the
gas film using a locally planar Stokes-flow solution with Maxwell
velocity-jump conditions at the oil-air and air-water interfaces.

The continuum gas response is coupled to:

- the leading spherical Stokes response inside the oil droplet;
- the leading spherical response in the surrounding water;
- the compound oil-core and gas-shell buoyancy; and
- the finite-Reynolds-number Feng drag correction.

### Main continuum solver

```text
TP_Cont/RA_ThreePhaseContinuumModel.m
```

### Principal continuum functions

- `RA_Continuum_BuildFixedState.m`  
  Calculates properties and quantities independent of gas-layer
  thickness.

- `RA_Continuum_BuildGasResponse.m`  
  Constructs the continuum gas-film flux and traction response.

- `RA_Continuum_ApplyGasClosure.m`  
  Applies the selected pressure-gradient-free or zero-relative-flux
  closure.

- `RA_Continuum_SolveLiquidCoupling.m`  
  Solves for the oil-air and air-water interfacial velocity amplitudes.

- `RA_Continuum_CalculateDragAndBuoyancy.m`  
  Calculates drag, buoyancy, and force-balance quantities.

- `RA_Continuum_EvaluateThickness.m`  
  Evaluates one trial gas-layer thickness.

- `RA_Continuum_ScanResidual.m`  
  Scans the thickness-dependent force residual.

- `RA_Continuum_SolveBrackets.m`  
  Solves the identified root brackets.

- `RA_Continuum_ReconstructVelocityProfile.m`  
  Reconstructs the continuum velocity profile across the gas layer.

- `RA_Continuum_PlotVelocityProfiles.m`  
  Plots the reconstructed gas velocity and its contributions.

- `RA_ThreePhaseContinuum_Benchmark.m`  
  Performs continuum-model verification and root-stability checks.

## L-BGK kinetic model

The three-phase L-BGK formulation replaces the continuum gas-film
response with a kinetic description while retaining the same spherical
oil and water coupling.

The L-BGK equation is solved only within the gas layer. The oil and
water remain continuum phases.

The kinetic solver calculates:

- the gas velocity distribution across the layer;
- the depth-integrated relative gas flux;
- the gas traction at the oil-air interface; and
- the gas traction at the air-water interface.

The validated implementation assumes fully diffuse molecular
accommodation at both gas-liquid interfaces.

### Main kinetic solver

```text
TP_LBGK/RA_ThreePhaseBGKModel.m
```

### Principal L-BGK functions

- `RA_BGK_SolvePlanarResponse.m`  
  Solves the locally planar L-BGK basis problems at a specified
  Knudsen number.

- `RA_BGK_GaussHermite.m`  
  Generates the Gauss-Hermite molecular-velocity nodes and weights.

- `RA_BGK_BuildResponseTable.m`  
  Constructs the kinetic response table over a Knudsen-number grid.

- `RA_BGK_LoadOrBuildResponseTable.m`  
  Loads an existing response table or builds a new one.

- `RA_BGK_InterpolateResponse.m`  
  Interpolates the kinetic response at a trial Knudsen number.

- `RA_BGK_ApplyGasClosure.m`  
  Applies the selected pressure-gradient-free or zero-relative-flux
  closure.

- `RA_BGK_SolveLiquidCoupling.m`  
  Couples the kinetic gas tractions to the oil and water responses.

- `RA_BGK_EvaluateThickness.m`  
  Evaluates one trial gas-layer thickness.

- `RA_BGK_SolveThickness.m`  
  performs the gas-layer-thickness inversion.

- `RA_BGK_PlotVelocityProfiles.m`  
  Reconstructs and plots the gas velocity across the layer.

- `RA_BGK_PlotSphericalVelocityReconstruction.m`  
  Maps the locally planar tangential velocity onto the leading
  spherical harmonic for visual interpretation. This reconstruction
  does not resolve radial gas velocity or global streamlines.

## Running the models

The MATLAB code cannot be run directly from the GitHub webpage. The
repository must first be downloaded or cloned and then opened locally
in MATLAB.

### 1. Open the repository in MATLAB

Set the repository root as the current MATLAB folder.

For example, if the repository has been downloaded to:

```text
C:\Research\gas-mediated-droplet-mobility
```

set that folder as the current MATLAB folder.

### 2. Add the model folders to the MATLAB path

Run:

```matlab
modelRoot = fullfile(pwd,'three_phase_models');

addpath(modelRoot);
addpath(fullfile(modelRoot,'shared'));
addpath(fullfile(modelRoot,'TP_Cont'));
addpath(fullfile(modelRoot,'TP_LBGK'));

rehash;
```

Alternatively:

```matlab
addpath(genpath(fullfile(pwd,'three_phase_models')));
rehash;
```

The explicit path commands are preferable because they make the
required folders clear.

## Model assumptions

The principal assumptions common to the continuum and L-BGK models are:

- the measured diameter is interpreted as the outer air-water
  diameter;
- the oil core and gas shell are spherical;
- the gas layer has a model-inferred uniform equivalent thickness;
- the gas layer is locally planar because its thickness is much
  smaller than the droplet radius;
- the oil and water responses retain their leading spherical modes;
- the liquid interfaces are mobile;
- tangential momentum transfer is resolved separately at the oil-air
  and air-water interfaces;
- normal gas transfer is not solved within the instantaneous
  hydrodynamic calculation;
- gas-layer growth is treated separately by the convective
  Epstein-Plesset model;
- the L-BGK implementation currently assumes fully diffuse molecular
  accommodation;
- global angular variations in gas-layer thickness, gas accumulation,
  and gas release are not spatially resolved.

## Interpretation of the two closures

The pressure-gradient-free and zero-relative-flux formulations are not
different accuracy levels of the same closure. They represent different
assumptions about global gas transport.

### Pressure-gradient-free closure

```text
G = 0
```

- permits nonzero tangential gas transport;
- produces equal gas traction at the two boundaries in the continuum
  limit;
- may represent local transport accompanied by redistribution
  elsewhere around the droplet.

### Zero-relative-flux closure

```text
q_relative = 0
```

- determines the pressure gradient required to cancel the relative gas
  flux;
- can produce unequal gas tractions at the two boundaries;
- represents a steady, uniform attached-shell limiting case.

The experimental measurements do not directly distinguish between
these global limits.

Generated files are not required source code. They may be regenerated

## Troubleshooting

### MATLAB cannot find a function

Run:

```matlab
addpath(genpath(fullfile(pwd,'three_phase_models')));
rehash;
which RA_ThreePhaseBGKModel -all
which RA_ThreePhaseContinuumModel -all
```

Confirm that MATLAB points to the files in this repository.

### An old model version is being called

Run:

```matlab
clear functions;
restoredefaultpath;
rehash toolboxcache;
```

Then add only the folders from this repository.

### XSteam is not found

Confirm that:

```text
three_phase_models/shared/XSteam.m
```

exists and that `shared` has been added to the MATLAB path.

### No gas-layer-thickness root is found

Inspect:

- the trial-thickness range;
- the residual scan;
- the oil and water properties;
- the measured velocity and diameter;
- the selected closure;
- the L-BGK response-table range; and
- the validity flags returned by the model.
